What we help with

  • Grab bar installation: bathroom, shower, tub, toilet and entry areas, mounted into wall studs or manufacturer-rated anchors
  • Wheelchair & walker adjustments: manual chairs and walkers: brakes, footrests, armrests, loose hardware and tire pressure
  • Lever door handles: swap round knobs for easy-grip levers
  • Lower closet rods & shelves: bring everyday items back within reach
  • Bathroom safety setup: toilet safety frames, raised seats, shower chairs and transfer benches
  • Clear, well-lit paths: secure or remove loose rugs, tidy cords, rearrange furniture for walker and wheelchair space, add night lights and brighter bulbs
  • Loose handrails: tighten existing handrails and stair rails
  • Home safety walk-through: we look for trip hazards and hard-to-reach spots, then fix what we can on the same visit

A few helpful answers

What does it cost to install grab bars?

Labor is $70 per person per hour, or $45 at the Community Rate, with a two-hour minimum for the first person ($140 standard or $90 Community Rate). One or two grab bars usually fit in that two-hour visit. See typical times in our job time guide. Before we start, you approve an estimate and a not-to-exceed cap. Bars and hardware are separate: use your own, or we pick up the ones you choose at documented actual cost.

Can you install grab bars on tile or a fiberglass shower?

Tile over a solid wall usually works with the right bit and care. Fiberglass and acrylic tub surrounds need special anchors made for hollow walls, so we review the wall first and tell you what we recommend. We follow the bar maker’s mounting instructions and won’t install a bar we can’t mount securely.

Do you repair power wheelchairs or scooters?

No. We adjust and tighten manual wheelchairs and walkers: brakes, footrests, armrests, loose hardware and tire pressure. Power wheelchairs, scooters, lift-chair electronics and anything under warranty should go to the supplier or manufacturer.

Good to know before we visit

This is practical home help, not a medical or occupational therapy assessment. For a clinical home-safety evaluation, ask your doctor about an occupational therapist referral. We don’t build ramps, install stair lifts or chairlifts, or do plumbing, electrical or work that needs a trade license or permit. Work stays at ground level or within our five-foot working-height limit.
